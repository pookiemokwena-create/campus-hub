# AFTER THE LAST BELL
## Episode 1 — The Boy Who Borrowed Tomorrow
*Original supernatural adventure anime • Set in Johannesburg*

### COLD OPEN

**EXT. JOHANNESBURG — NIGHT**

The city hangs upside down.

Cars float above the rooftops. Rain climbs toward the clouds. A minibus taxi drifts past a billboard advertising tomorrow’s weather.

On the underside of a pedestrian bridge, **LEBO MOKOENA, 17**, clings to the railing with one hand.

His other hand holds a cracked brass watch.

A girl in a red school blazer stands on the bridge as though gravity still works. Her shadow points toward him, against the light.

**GIRL**  
I told you not to wish for a better day.

Lebo looks down—up—at the impossible city.

**LEBO**  
In my defence, this is definitely different.

The watch strikes thirteen.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

**TITLE: AFTER THE LAST BELL**

### SCENE 1 — SEVEN HOURS EARLIER

**INT. KITCHEN — AFTERNOON**

A kettle whistles. Lebo sits at a tiny table, trying to repair the zip on his school bag with a fork.

His little sister, **NALedi, 10**, watches him over a slice of toast.

**NALEDI**  
You know the fork is losing?

The zip breaks completely.

**LEBO**  
It was a strategic retreat.

She slides a folded paper star across the table.

**NALEDI**  
For luck. You promised you’d help with my science thing when you got back.

**LEBO**  
Solar system. Nine planets. Easy.

**NALEDI**  
Eight.

**LEBO**  
One already escaped. Smart.

He slips the star into his blazer pocket.

At the doorway, he checks a message on his battered phone:

**FINAL SCHOLARSHIP INTERVIEW — 15:00. LATE ARRIVALS CANNOT BE ADMITTED.**

The kitchen clock reads **14:12**.

Then stops.

Lebo taps its glass.

It starts again.

### SCENE 2 — THE WRONG SHORTCUT

**EXT. BRAAMFONTEIN — AFTERNOON**

Lebo runs through busy pavements, one arm wrapped around his broken bag.

His best friend, **TUMI, 17**, speaks through his phone.

**TUMI (PHONE)**  
Walk in like they already gave you the scholarship.

**LEBO**  
At this rate I’m walking in tomorrow.

**TUMI (PHONE)**  
Then act like they invited you tomorrow.

Lebo turns into an alley.

The street noise cuts out.

Not fades. Cuts.

At the far end sits a watch-repair stall beneath a yellow umbrella. Hundreds of watches hang from its ribs.

None of their hands move.

An elderly woman behind the stall looks up before he speaks.

**WATCH SELLER**  
You’re late.

**LEBO**  
Apparently that’s public information.

She places a brass watch on the counter.

Its face has thirteen marks.

**WATCH SELLER**  
One minute. Yours to repeat.

**LEBO**  
How much?

**WATCH SELLER**  
A minute.

He gives her a tired smile.

**LEBO**  
I actually don’t have one to spare.

He turns to leave. His torn bag catches on the stall. Papers spill across the pavement.

The woman helps gather them. She pauses over a drawing Naledi has tucked between his school reports: two stick figures standing on Saturn.

**WATCH SELLER**  
Someone is expecting you home.

**LEBO**  
Someone wants me to glue balls onto wire.

She places the papers in his arms. The watch rests on top.

**WATCH SELLER**  
Then don’t spend more than you can afford.

A taxi hoots behind him.

Lebo glances toward the sound.

When he looks back, the stall is gone.

### SCENE 3 — ONE GOOD MINUTE

**INT. SCHOLARSHIP OFFICE — AFTERNOON**

**15:01.**

A receptionist closes a folder.

**RECEPTIONIST**  
I’m sorry. The panel has started its final review.

Lebo stands breathless in front of her desk.

Through a glass partition he can see three interviewers. An empty chair. A jug of water.

**LEBO**  
Please. I’m right here.

Her expression softens, but her hand stays on the folder.

The watch grows warm in his pocket.

Across its glass, tiny letters emerge:

**TURN BACK.**

He turns the crown.

The room folds like paper.

Water rises from a glass into the jug. A printer swallows a sheet. The receptionist’s words slide backward into her mouth.

Lebo stands outside the office.

**15:00.**

He opens the door.

**RECEPTIONIST**  
Lebo Mokoena?

He nods, stunned.

**RECEPTIONIST**  
Just in time.

For the first time all day, he smiles.

Behind him, reflected in the glass door, someone smiles back.

Tall. Faceless.

Wearing his school uniform.

### SCENE 4 — THE GIRL WITH THE WRONG SHADOW

**EXT. OFFICE BUILDING — LATE AFTERNOON**

Lebo bursts out onto the pavement and sends Tumi a voice note.

**LEBO**  
I think I actually did it. I answered the maths question. All of it. Even the part with letters pretending to be numbers.

A girl in a red blazer blocks his path.

**AYA, 17.** Close-cropped hair. A silver safety pin through her collar. A black case slung over her shoulder.

Her shadow moves a fraction of a second after she does.

**AYA**  
Show me your hands.

**LEBO**  
Usually people start with hello.

She catches his wrist.

A thin black ring circles his finger, just beneath the skin.

Aya releases him.

**AYA**  
You used it.

Lebo closes his hand over the watch in his pocket.

**LEBO**  
Do you know that woman?

**AYA**  
How many turns?

**LEBO**  
One.

Aya looks past him at the office door.

In its reflection, the faceless student stands directly behind Lebo.

On the pavement, nothing is there.

**AYA**  
Good. We can still—

Every phone on the street rings.

One note.

Like a school bell.

Aya goes very still.

**AYA**  
Don’t answer yours.

Lebo’s screen lights up.

**NALEDI CALLING.**

### SCENE 5 — COLLECTION

**EXT. CITY STREET — CONTINUOUS**

The phone keeps ringing.

**LEBO**  
It’s my sister.

**AYA**  
Not anymore.

He answers.

Silence.

Then his own voice, older and exhausted:

**VOICE (PHONE)**  
Whatever she tells you, don’t let her take the watch.

The call ends.

Across the street, a taxi freezes in mid-turn.

A dropped coin stops above the pavement.

Everyone goes still except Lebo and Aya.

The faceless student steps out of the office window.

Glass stretches around it like water.

It lands barefoot on the pavement. Its uniform drips black sand.

Where its face should be, an hour hand turns beneath pale skin.

**COLLECTOR**  
One minute borrowed.

Its head tilts toward Lebo.

**COLLECTOR**  
One tomorrow due.

Aya drops her case and opens it with her heel.

Inside lies a straight, narrow sword. Its blade resembles the minute hand of an enormous clock.

She draws it.

**LEBO**  
You brought a sword to school?

**AYA**  
I brought homework too. It’s been a disappointing day.

The Collector lunges.

Aya blocks. The impact sounds like a thousand alarm clocks.

Windows spiderweb down the street.

**AYA**  
Run!

Lebo runs.

Three steps.

Then he notices the Collector’s shadow stretching toward a frozen little boy beside the taxi.

The shadow wraps around the boy’s ankle.

His shoe begins to crumble into dust.

Lebo stops.

**LEBO**  
What’s it doing?

Aya strains against the blade.

**AYA**  
Taking the nearest tomorrow if it can’t get yours!

Lebo looks at the watch.

**AYA**  
Don’t—!

He turns the crown.

### SCENE 6 — THE SECOND TURN

The world snaps backward.

Lebo stands where he answered the phone.

It rings in his hand.

This time, he throws it into an open rubbish bin.

**LEBO**  
The kid by the taxi! Left ankle!

Aya doesn’t question him.

She turns, draws, and pins the boy’s shadow to the road with her sword.

The Collector tears out of the window, shrieking.

A second black ring appears on Lebo’s hand.

The watch cracks.

Above the rooftops, an enormous clock face becomes visible through the clouds.

Its hands begin moving backward.

Aya looks up.

For the first time, she looks frightened.

**AYA**  
That isn’t supposed to happen.

The ground kicks beneath them.

Cars lift from the road.

Rain begins to rise.

### SCENE 7 — THE CITY ABOVE

**EXT. PEDESTRIAN BRIDGE — NIGHT THAT ARRIVES TOO QUICKLY**

Lebo slides along the pavement as gravity changes direction.

Aya grabs his sleeve, dragging him toward a bridge railing.

He catches it just as the street drops away into the sky.

Now we are back at the opening.

The upside-down city. The drifting taxi. Aya standing where standing should be impossible.

**AYA**  
I told you not to wish for a better day.

**LEBO**  
In my defence, this is definitely different.

The watch strikes thirteen.

The Collector climbs toward them along the side of a floating bus.

Lebo’s fingers begin to slip.

Something falls out of his pocket.

Naledi’s paper star.

He snatches it with the hand holding the watch.

One edge unfolds.

Inside, in his sister’s careful handwriting:

**COME HOME, IDIOT.**

Lebo laughs once, shakily.

Then the watch opens.

There are no gears inside.

Only a small, bright window.

Through it, he sees his kitchen.

Naledi sits at the table with eight painted planets.

A shadow stands behind her.

Lebo stops breathing.

**COLLECTOR**  
Payment accepted.

The window goes dark.

Lebo looks up at Aya.

**LEBO**  
Tell me how to get her back.

Aya looks at the paper star. Then at the two rings on his hand.

She offers him her sword, hilt first.

**AYA**  
First, you have to let go.

Below him, far beyond the floating city, a door opens in the sky.

Someone on the other side is knocking.

Lebo releases the railing.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

### END CREDITS

*Visual: Eight paper planets turn slowly above an empty kitchen chair. With each revolution, one casts the shadow of a person instead of a sphere.*

### POST-CREDITS

**INT. WATCH-REPAIR SHOP — UNKNOWN TIME**

The old woman hangs a newly repaired watch beneath her yellow umbrella.

A man sits across from her. His right hand is covered in black rings.

We do not see his face.

**WATCH SELLER**  
You called him?

**MAN**  
He never listens.

**WATCH SELLER**  
Neither did you, Lebo.

On the counter lies a faded photograph of Lebo, Naledi, and Aya.

Someone has scratched Aya’s face out.

**END OF EPISODE 1**
