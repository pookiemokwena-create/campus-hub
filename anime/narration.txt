After the Last Bell. Episode One: The Boy Who Borrowed Tomorrow.
Above Johannesburg, cars floated past the rooftops. Rain climbed into the clouds. Lebo hung from a bridge with one hand. In the other, he held a cracked brass watch. A girl in a red blazer stood where gravity said she shouldn't. I told you not to wish for a better day, she said.
Seven hours earlier, the world still made sense. Mostly.
Lebo sat in his kitchen, repairing his school bag with a fork. His little sister, Naledi, watched the zip break. You know the fork is losing, she said. Strategic retreat, Lebo replied.
She gave him a folded paper star. For luck. And don't forget my solar system project.
Lebo tucked the star into his blazer. On his phone waited a scholarship interview reminder. Three o'clock. No late arrivals. This was his chance to change things for both of them.
He ran into the afternoon. His best friend Tumi called with advice. Walk in like they already gave you the scholarship. At this rate, Lebo said, I'm walking in tomorrow.
He took a shortcut into an alley. Suddenly, every sound in the city stopped.
===
Beneath a yellow umbrella, an old woman sat surrounded by watches. Not one was ticking.
You're late, she said. Apparently that's public information, Lebo answered.
She placed a brass watch on the counter. Its face had thirteen marks. One minute, she told him. Yours to repeat.
How much? A minute.
He turned to leave, but his broken bag caught the stall. His papers scattered. The woman helped gather them. Among the school reports was Naledi's drawing: two stick figures standing on Saturn.
Someone is expecting you home, the woman said. Then don't spend more than you can afford.
She pressed the watch into his hand. A taxi hooted. Lebo looked away for a moment. When he looked back, the stall was gone.
He reached the interview at one minute past three. The receptionist closed her folder. I'm sorry. The panel has started its final review.
In his pocket, the watch grew warm. Two words appeared across the glass. Turn back.
Lebo turned the crown.
Water rose into a jug. A printer swallowed a page. And suddenly he stood outside the office again. Three o'clock exactly.
Just in time, the receptionist said.
Lebo smiled. In the glass behind him, a faceless figure wearing his uniform smiled back.
===
The interview went better than he had dared to hope. Outside, Lebo was already recording a message to Tumi when a girl in a red blazer blocked his path.
Her name was Aya. Her shadow moved a moment after she did.
Show me your hands, she said. Beneath the skin of Lebo's finger was a thin black ring.
How many turns? Just one.
Then every phone on the street rang like a school bell.
Don't answer yours, Aya warned.
But the screen said Naledi. Lebo answered.
The voice wasn't his sister's. It was his own. Older. Exhausted. Whatever she tells you, don't let her take the watch.
The call ended. Traffic froze. A dropped coin stopped above the road.
Something stepped out of a glass window. A faceless student, dripping black sand. One minute borrowed, it whispered. One tomorrow due.
Aya opened her case and drew a sword shaped like the hand of a clock. Run!
Lebo took three steps. Then he saw the creature's shadow reaching for a little boy beside a taxi. The child's shoe began crumbling to dust.
It takes the nearest tomorrow if it can't get yours, Aya shouted.
Lebo looked at the watch. He could run. He could save himself.
Instead, he turned the crown again.
===
The phone rang in his hand. This time, Lebo threw it away. The boy by the taxi! Left ankle!
Aya moved without questioning him. Her blade pinned the creature's shadow to the street.
A second black ring appeared on Lebo's hand. The watch cracked.
Above the city, an enormous clock emerged through the clouds. Its hands moved backward. Aya looked up in fear. That isn't supposed to happen.
The road tilted. Cars lifted. Rain rose. Aya caught Lebo's sleeve and pulled him toward a bridge. He grabbed the railing as the city fell into the sky.
Now he was hanging over everything he had ever known.
I told you not to wish for a better day, Aya said. In my defence, Lebo answered, this is definitely different.
The watch struck thirteen.
Naledi's paper star slipped from his pocket. He caught it. Inside were three words in her careful handwriting. Come home, idiot.
The watch opened. There were no gears. Only a window into his kitchen. Naledi sat beside her painted planets. A shadow stood behind her.
Payment accepted, whispered the creature. The window went dark.
Tell me how to get her back, Lebo said.
Aya offered him her sword. First, you have to let go.
Far beneath the floating city, a door opened in the sky.
Lebo released the railing.
